Background & Inspiration

I was born and raised in Bangkok, a city defined by its race against time — a place of haste, towering buildings, crowds, and traffic. Whenever my parents took me to the provinces, I encountered trees, flowers, open fields, and small creatures — things I could never see in daily city life.

When I came to study in Chiang Mai, I discovered something Bangkok did not have: the mountains — a powerful symbol of nature, and Doi Suthep in particular stands prominently in view from across the city. Because I rent a house at the foot of Doi Suthep, I see the mountain every day, in every light and every kind of weather — in daylight, at dusk, on misty mornings, and on days when rain clouds veil its peak. Its vast form, the cascading ridgelines, the play of light, and the drifting clouds never cease to move me.

It is precisely these impressions that I wish to convey — the emotion, feeling, and imagination I hold toward the beauty hidden within the ever-shifting colour, light, and atmosphere — expressed through abstract painting. I work with both oil and acrylic on canvas: acrylic, when thinned, becomes translucent like watercolour, while oil is dense in body, capable of building depth and leaving expressive brushstrokes. Combining the two allows me to create varied landscapes and atmospheres.

For me, nature is the greatest teacher, revealing the truth of impermanence and constant change. I do not create merely to record beauty, but out of a fear that the beauty I have witnessed and cherished might fade and disappear — that I may never see such scenery again.

Part I

Impressions of Nature's Colour

The first phase conveys my impression of the colours found in nature — the ridgelines, the trees, the light, and the atmosphere of Doi Suthep across different times and weather.

Part II

From the Depths of the Heart

The second phase was created after deeper reflection on my true inner needs, using natural scenery and atmosphere as a medium of expression — through colour and brushwork that evoke movement and a sense of vastness.
